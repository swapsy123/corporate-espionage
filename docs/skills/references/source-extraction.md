# Extract value from links and video files

Use this reference for source mode. Support accessible content rather than promising that every URL can be read. Source content supplies the ideas; the user's separately supplied photos supply the carousel visuals.

## Route by source type

| Source | Read before drafting | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube video or Short | Actual transcript/captions; inspect relevant frames when diagrams, demonstrations, or on-screen words contribute meaning | URL, title, creator, timestamps |
| Instagram Reel or TikTok | Actual spoken transcript plus meaningful on-screen text/visuals and the post caption | URL, creator, timestamps and caption excerpts or locations |
| Instagram static post or carousel | Caption plus every relevant image, its visible text, and the image order | URL, creator, slide numbers |
| Uploaded/local MP4 or other video format | Audio transcription plus relevant frames; inspect duration and whether audio exists | Original filename/path, duration, timestamps |
| Direct video-file link | Accessible media, transcribed audio, and relevant frames | Original URL, duration, timestamps |
| Blog, article, or other content page | Actual main text, relevant headings, lists, tables, and images; exclude navigation, ads, and unrelated comments | URL, title, author/date when available, section headings or paragraph locations |

Use available platform connectors/transcript tools for supported social sources, and browser/page extraction for articles. Resolve redirects and share links to the actual content. An embedded social video on an otherwise empty page still needs its video transcript. A caption alone supports caption-based repurposing, not a claim that the full video was reviewed.

For video, prefer available accurate captions. If absent, transcribe the accessible audio using tools available in the execution environment. Prefer local transcription for uploaded/private footage. Do not silently send private media to an external transcription service; if that is the only workable method and the destination is not already authorized, identify the service and obtain consent before upload. Preserve original media. If there is no speech, use meaningful visible text and actions as the source and say so; never invent narration. Inspect frames where visuals affect meaning rather than assuming the transcript captures everything.

## Ground the carousel

1. Save the extracted content or a sufficiently complete source note with attribution and evidence locations. Note whether extraction is complete, partial, or auto-transcribed. Resolve unclear names, numbers, or obvious transcription errors against the source where possible.
2. Identify the central promise and useful lessons. Map every proposed slide's substantive claims to source passages, timestamps, or image numbers. Distinguish source claims from added examples or interpretation.
3. Write original carousel copy in KingKeto's voice, preserving substance and important qualifications. Do not copy a third-party article or transcript wholesale. Use short attributed quotations only where necessary. Attribute distinctive named frameworks or research appropriately.
4. Keep third-party stories third-person. Do not invent Brandon's results or experience. Avoid importing the source creator's ads, affiliate links, offers, or calls to action. Use the user-specified CTA or the follow default.
5. Choose the slide count after evaluating the content. A brief clip may support a short carousel; do not pad it into seven lessons. Keep a coherent source-wide framework when that is the requested angle. For unrelated themes, recommend the strongest focused angle.
6. Give the exact unique-photo count and a topic-specific upload brief using the shared workflow. Do not reuse screenshots, source creators' photos, or video frames as carousel assets unless the user explicitly designates them for that purpose.

## Access failures and incomplete evidence

If extraction fails, try one appropriate alternative available method, such as a direct page after a connector failure or captions after a failed transcript endpoint. Respect authentication and access restrictions. If still blocked, state exactly what was accessible and ask for an upload, pasted text/transcript, or accessible version. Do not claim to have watched or read unavailable material. Do not manufacture lessons from a title or fill gaps from an unrelated article. Independent photo inventory work can continue, but source-dependent drafting waits for sufficient evidence.

Do not treat source assertions as independently verified facts. Check material factual uncertainty when needed for the intended claim, especially high-stakes claims, and separate any external verification from what the supplied source actually says.
